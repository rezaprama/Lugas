Making an academic claim

Permanent link

Practical guidance to state a contestable proposition with clear scope and an appropriate level of confidence, with wording aligned to rhetorical purpose and available evidence.

Original example

Rhetorical objective
State a contestable proposition with clear scope and an appropriate level of confidence.

Build relations, not rhetorical force

An academic argument earns a claim through reasons, evidence, and a warrant that explains their relation. Counterarguments and concessions should change the reasoning, not serve as decorative balance. Use Rhetorical Moves to inspect paragraph function.

Reasoning boundary

Confident wording cannot substitute for a missing warrant. A conclusion should not broaden the evidence, population, or causal force established in the argument.
