Establishing a research context

Permanent link

Practical guidance to place the study within a bounded scholarly, social, or practical setting, with wording aligned to rhetorical purpose and available evidence.

Original example

Rhetorical objective
Place the study within a bounded scholarly, social, or practical setting.

Frame a problem the study can answer

Research framing connects prior knowledge to the decision made by the present study. A strong frame distinguishes importance from evidence, turns a literature-based uncertainty into a specific problem, and keeps the aim aligned with the design. Read Research Gap for a focused workflow.

Evidence boundary

Claims about novelty, absence, prevalence, urgency, and impact require support. Do not use a broad field-level claim to justify a study whose sample or method addresses only a narrow question.
