Association vs causation

Permanent link

Practical guidance to distinguish observed covariation from evidence that changing one factor changes another, with wording aligned to rhetorical purpose and available evidence.

Original example

Income and tree cover were associated in the survey; the analysis does not establish that income caused the observed difference.

Rhetorical objective
Distinguish observed covariation from evidence that changing one factor changes another.
Use cases
  • results interpretation
  • abstracts
  • press-facing summaries
Related phrase functions
distinguish_causation, describe_association
Related skills
Correlation language
Evidence and claim warning
Causal verbs require a credible causal design; association, prediction, significance, and null results do not establish causation.

Control the proposition, not only the tone

Claim control protects scope, modality, frequency, comparison, uncertainty, and causal status. A hedge cannot repair a mismatched relation. Read Association vs Causation and Preserve Claim Strength.

Causal boundary

May cause remains causal. Use causal language only when the design and identification assumptions support a counterfactual claim.
