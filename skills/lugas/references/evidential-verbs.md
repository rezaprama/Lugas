# Evidential Verbs and Claim Control

Choose a verb from the relation the evidence supports, not from its apparent formality. Verb force depends on subject, object, syntax, design, and qualification; this is a routing guide, not a universal ladder.

## Source attribution

- `reports`, `describes`, `states`, and `argues` keep ownership with a cited source.
- `defines` attributes a particular definition; it does not make that definition universal.
- `claims` can sound distancing or adversarial. Use it only when that stance is intended.

## Empirical reporting

- `observed`, `measured`, `estimated`, and `found` report what an analysis produced.
- `was associated with`, `co-varied with`, and `predicted` describe statistical relations without intervention effects.
- `differed`, `increased`, and `decreased` need an explicit comparator, direction, scale, and time frame where relevant.

## Interpretation

- `may suggest`, `is compatible with`, and `is consistent with` keep alternatives open. They do not make weak evidence strong.
- `supports` requires evidence that bears on the stated proposition; it does not mean proves.
- `indicates` can read strongly. Use it for a direct measured result or add the boundary that makes its force clear.
- `demonstrates`, `establishes`, and `confirms` are usually too strong for a single study unless the proposition, design, and evidential standard are unusually well defined.

## Causal language

Use `caused`, `led to`, `resulted in`, `increased`, `reduced`, or `had an effect on` causally only when the design identifies the relevant counterfactual effect and the assumptions are stated. Adjustment, temporal order, prediction, or a plausible mechanism alone is insufficient.

When causal identification is absent, prefer an explicit association plus the unresolved causal limitation. Read [claim-strength.md](claim-strength.md) for the full gate.

## Quick audit

For each evidential verb, ask:

1. Who owns the proposition: the current authors, a cited source, participants, or the analysis?
2. What relation is asserted: observation, pattern, association, prediction, interpretation, or cause?
3. What design and evidence justify that relation?
4. What population, condition, uncertainty, and alternative explanation bound it?

Repair the verb or the surrounding qualification when any answer is missing. Never invent evidence to preserve a preferred verb.
